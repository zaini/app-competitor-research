# Estimation methods

Every number in the report should say where it came from. Use ranges and plain words such as "order of magnitude".

## Downloads and revenue

- AppKittie's monthly downloads and revenue are modelled estimates. For many apps, revenue = downloads × a round multiplier, so two apps with similar downloads can look 10× apart only because they got different multipliers.
- If the user shares real numbers, compare them with AppKittie's numbers for their own app and state the ratio (e.g. "AppKittie's revenue for you was within 15% of real MRR, but downloads were ~2× too high"). Apply that lesson to competitors in words, not by rewriting their numbers.
- Cross-checks: rating count growth (AppKittie review history), chart positions (Trending), and how many reviews per month the public feed shows.

## Meta ad spend

1. **When AppKittie has transparency data** (the Ad Spend tab, or a "Meta (EU)" spend figure): AppKittie reports observed EU/UK spend plus a modelled US share. Treat its 30-day total as a floor-ish estimate of worldwide spend: show **1.0×–2.5×** that figure. Apps that mostly target the US, Middle East or Asia sit at the high end.
   - If the user shares their real Meta spend by country, calibrate: real worldwide spend ÷ AppKittie's figure for their app gives a multiplier. In testing, AppKittie captured roughly 70–75% of real worldwide Meta spend for an app that mostly advertised outside the EU.
2. **When AppKittie has nothing**: count active ads in the Meta Ad Library (US, UK and other key markets) and multiply by **$50–$250 per active ad per month**. That is the range seen where both numbers were known. Big, always-on advertisers sit at the low end per ad (many small variants); small apps with a few ads sit at the high end.
3. Say clearly that creator, influencer and UGC deals don't appear anywhere, and that for creator-led apps they can be the main spend.

## Apple Search Ads

Apple publishes no spend. AppKittie's "Apple Ads" count is the number of creatives in Apple's EU ad repository (roughly one per ad variant per country). Use tiers, then cap with a downloads check:

| Creatives | Read | Monthly spend range |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | not running (or not in the EU) | – |
| 1–20 | small test | $100–$1.5k |
| 20–200 | established programme | $2k–$20k |
| 200–2,000 | heavy | $15k–$120k |
| 2,000+ | very heavy | $100k+ |

Downloads check: paid search rarely drives more than 15–35% of iOS installs, at roughly $1–$3 per install in most non-game categories (more in finance/health). If the tier says more than downloads × 35% × $3, lower it.

Also note the countries the Apple Ads tab lists and how long ads have run; many countries and long runs mean an always-on programme.

## TikTok

AppKittie's TikTok transparency gives observed EU/UK spend and a modelled US share. Use the same 1.0×–2.5× range as Meta. TikTok's "reach" counts impressions, not people.

## Presenting estimates

One table per report: app, downloads*, revenue*, Meta evidence, Meta estimate per month, Apple Ads evidence, Apple Ads estimate per month, and a one-line read ("organic-led", "buying growth", "search-led"). Put the method in a collapsible "How these estimates are made" box under the table.
